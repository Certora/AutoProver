
It needs a nightly toolchain (for `cargo rustdoc --output-format json`) and a warm cargo registry.
Generated **2026-09-25**, against the releases
[`composer/spec/cvlr_reference.py`](../composer/spec/cvlr_reference.py) pins — `cvlr` 0.6.1,
`cvlr-solana` 0.5.0 and their family. It is meant to shrink, and the counts are the measure.

---

## Why this list exists

AutoProver builds a retrieval corpus of the CVLR API from the crates' own rustdoc
([`docs/cvlr-api-docs-plan.md`](./cvlr-api-docs-plan.md)). rustdoc supplies **existence, signature,
defining crate and feature gate** with no doc comment at all, and by the tool census of the one
recorded live run, that is most of what an authoring agent asks for. What it cannot supply is
**what an item is for** — that has to be written by a person, in the crate, and 77% of the surface
has nobody's answer.

This matters beyond AutoProver. The same gap is what a human reading docs.rs hits.

**The macros are the sharp end**, and they are why this is on AutoProver's critical path. CVLR's
authoring agent currently recovers a macro's contract by reading the crate source, which is going
away; after that, an undocumented macro has no answer anywhere. Two macro facts decided verdicts in
the recorded run — that `cvlr_assert_le!`/`cvlr_assert_eq!` log both operands, and that
`cvlr-solana`'s `invoke!`/`invoke_signed!` are program-side shadowing macros with no CPI model
behind them. Neither is inferable from a signature, and neither is written down.

One practical note for `cvlr-asserts`: eighteen of its macros (`cvlr_assert_le`, `cvlr_assume_lt`
and siblings) are generated by `impl_bin_assert!` / `impl_bin_assume!` / `impl_bin_assert_if!`, so
there is no source line to attach a `///` to. A `#[doc = concat!(...)]` on the template works and
rustdoc carries the rendered text through per generated macro — verified against
`--output-format json`, schema 60. Three edits cover eighteen items.
